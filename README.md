# music-video-pipeline

[![CI](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

Turn a master audio track, a lyrics file, cast reference photos, and a narrative
concept into a complete, lip-synced music video — driven end-to-end against a
local [ComfyUI](https://github.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI) instance running
MiniMax H3.

This README, [`CLAUDE.md`](./CLAUDE.md), and the docs under [`docs/`](./docs/)
describe what's actually built and are kept code-accurate — see "Known gaps"
below for where reality still falls short of the design. The installed command
is `music-video-maker`; the authoring tool is `mvm-author`.

Everything here was built privately first, and the docs cite issue numbers
(`#26`, `#47`, `#58`…) as the receipts for rules that look arbitrary until you
know what they cost. Those issues are preserved here: the eleven still open
carry their full text, and the rest are kept as closed titles, so every
reference resolves to the thing it names.

## Why this isn't a button

MiniMax H3 is a genuinely remarkable model. Hand it a photo, a line of text and
some audio and it will give you a five-second clip with a recognisable person
lip-syncing in it. It is reasonable to look at that and conclude the music
video is a solved problem and the rest is glue.

This repository is the gap between that clip and a four-minute music video that
stays in sync, keeps the same cast, and tells one story. **Every item below is
something that actually went wrong here**, with an issue number attached — none
of it is hypothetical, and most of it was invisible until a full render was
watched end to end.

- **H3 renders shots, not songs.** Its trained range is 124–362 frames — about
  5 to 15 seconds. A 4:22 track is ~36 shots. Something has to decide where the
  cuts fall, and they have to land on lyric boundaries or the video cuts
  mid-word.
- **Lip-sync needs to know when each word is sung, and the model won't tell
  you.** That means forced alignment against the *real* lyrics, not ASR — ASR
  transcribes what it thinks it heard, and sung vocals are exactly what it is
  worst at. The lyrics are ground truth; alignment fits timestamps to them.
  Alignment can also fail badly while raising nothing, so it gets its own
  quality report (#35) and authored per-segment overrides for when no model is
  right (#42).
- **The frame count is quantized and the audio is not.** `length` is frames on
  a fixed `5 + 17k` grid at 24 fps. Ask for 6.0 s and you get 6.35 s. Per chunk
  that is imperceptible; across 36 chunks it is progressive lip-sync drift,
  because assembly muxes one master track over the concatenated result and
  never re-corrects a per-chunk mismatch (#20).
- **Every clip generates its own audio, and all of it must be thrown away.**
  Keep any of it and you get 36 differently-hallucinated fragments of a song
  instead of the song. Sync has to come from the alignment timestamps, not from
  generated sound.
- **The silent parts of the song are the easiest thing to forget.** Render only
  the voiced spans and every chunk looks perfect — but assembly concatenates
  end to end, so every instrumental gets squeezed out while the muxed master
  keeps it. A typical song is under half vocals. The failure is silent and
  total (#26).
- **Nothing carries between shots.** Each chunk is an independent diffusion
  sampling event. Consecutive shots re-invent the room, the wardrobe and the
  camera unless something deliberately bridges them — and the bridge has its
  own failure modes, below.
- **Chaining shots turns every per-chunk instruction into a recurrence
  relation.** This is the deepest lesson here and it cost three separate bugs.
  An instruction that is correct once can be catastrophic when applied to its
  own output: a reference photo wired as the "identity anchor" turned out to
  *mandate* the final frame, so every chained shot ended on a cover-cropped
  portrait (#44); "looking a few years younger" was re-applied to an
  already-younger frame once per shot until the lead became a different woman
  (#46); and a shot seeded from a frame showing the back of her head had no
  facial information at all, so the model invented a face when she turned round
  (#47). Ask of any new prompt component: *what does this do on the fifth
  application to its own output?*
- **A property that must hold across the whole video needs its own field.**
  Stated in forty places, it drifts. Geography wandered from a British terraced
  street into something reading as Central Park, every shot individually
  plausible and the sequence incoherent, because nothing named a place (#32).
  Presentation direction smuggled into a character's `role` is what made the
  performer sing through every instrumental, because `role` is injected into
  every chunk (#31).
- **The prompt's grammar decides what gets rendered.** H3 renders the
  grammatical subject of the sentence. "She walks past as the printer explodes"
  and "The printer explodes behind her as she walks past" are not the same
  request — the second one gets you an explosion. Found by A/B, and it is the
  single highest-leverage prompt rule in this project (#26).
- **Reference photos are conditioning, not identity.** They work on the
  reference path. The image-to-video node has no reference-image input *at
  all*, so on that path identity comes only from the seed frame — which means
  the seed frame's *content* silently decides whether the likeness survives
  (#47).
- **A two-hour render will be interrupted, and "resume" is harder than it
  sounds.** Reusing a chunk because its file exists proves the chunk *exists*,
  not that it is the *right* chunk. Edit the lyrics and every span moves while
  every file stays valid, and you assemble a silently desynced video with no
  error anywhere. So each chunk records a fingerprint of what it was rendered
  for — span, frames, resolution, prompt, seed, conditioning audio, text
  encoder, and a hash of the graph itself — and a resumed run compares (#34,
  #38, #39, #45).
- **The GPU is a shared, single resource, and taking it is a protocol.** The
  card has other tenants; one of them loads 15 GB on demand, so a quiet card is
  not a free card. Worse, when H3 stages its weights onto a contended card it
  does not reliably raise CUDA OOM — it can go silent mid-load and wedge the
  host hard enough to need a power cycle (#19, #23, #24).
- **The machine driving the render is a participant, not a spectator.** A
  laptop that sleeps mid-render freezes the orchestrator while the GPU box
  carries on finishing work nobody is listening for — observed at 3.5 hours
  against a 900-second watchdog, because a sleeping process gets no CPU and
  macOS's monotonic clock does not advance across sleep either (#43).
- **ComfyUI node IDs are not stable.** Anyone opening the workflow on the
  canvas renumbers them. Every node has to be located by `class_type` and
  title, or the pipeline injects the right value into the wrong node.

None of this is a criticism of H3 — most of it would apply to any video model
good enough to make the attempt worthwhile. It is what "just push a button"
turns out to mean once the button has to work 36 times in a row and produce
something a person will watch all the way through.

## What it does

The pipeline runs in five stages:

1. **Forced alignment** (`music_video_maker/alignment.py`) — `stable-ts`'s
   `model.align()` maps the master audio to the lyric text, producing
   word-level timestamps (`suppress_silence=True` for VAD, `regroup=True` for
   logical segments).
2. **Temporal slicing + prompt expansion** (`slicing.py`, `prompting.py`) —
   `pydub` slices per-segment audio stems that fit MiniMax H3's temporal
   context window (see [Chunk duration window](#chunk-duration-window)
   below); each chunk's prompt is a deterministic concatenation of Global
   Style + Narrative Concept + the active cast member's role + the literal
   lyric line.
3. **Asset staging** (`staging.py`) — cast images and audio stems are
   uploaded to ComfyUI's `POST /upload/image` (ComfyUI's single ingest
   endpoint for both images and audio); the pipeline always uses the
   server-returned filename, never the local one, in the workflow payload.
4. **Execution** (`workflow_graph.py`, `execution.py`, `resilience.py`) — the
   loaded `workflow_api.json` template is mutated per chunk, submitted to
   `POST /prompt` with a UUID4 `client_id`, and monitored over ComfyUI's
   WebSocket event stream (event-driven, no sleep-polling) until a matching
   `executing` message with `node == null` signals completion; the output is
   fetched through `/history/{prompt_id}` + `/view`.
5. **Assembly** (`assembly.py`) — FFmpeg's concat demuxer stitches the
   rendered chunk videos with `-c:v copy` (never re-encoded), strips every
   generated audio track, and muxes the pristine master audio track over the
   result.

Three deeper capabilities build on that base:

- **Character tags** (`lyrics.py`) — `[Name: Role]` tags in the lyrics file
  switch which cast member is "active" (and therefore whose reference photo
  and role text drive the prompt) for that line and every line after it,
  until the next tag. See [`docs/lyrics-format.md`](docs/lyrics-format.md).
- **I2V latent continuity** (`continuity.py`) — optionally bridges chunk
  *N-1*'s rendered last frame into chunk *N*'s render as a seed image, so
  consecutive shots don't visually re-layout themselves on every lyric line.
  This renders through a second, separately authored workflow template
  (`MiniMaxH3ImageToVideo`), never a mutation of the base one. See
  [`docs/workflow-template-guide.md`](docs/workflow-template-guide.md).
- **Fault-tolerant execution + hardware profiles** (`resilience.py`,
  `hardware.py`) — a watchdog/retry/dead-letter state machine so one chunk's
  failure never kills the run, plus static per-GPU chunk-duration and
  optimization-node profiles.

## Non-negotiable invariants

These explain most of the design choices above, so they're worth stating up
front:

- **Lyrics are immutable truth.** Stage 1 is forced alignment only — it never
  runs ASR/transcription of the vocals. The lyric text file is the ground
  truth timestamps are fit *to*, not text the model is asked to guess.
- **The master audio track is the only audio in the final video.** Every
  chunk's own generated audio (even MiniMax H3's own audio VAE output) is
  discarded at assembly time; lip-sync comes entirely from the Stage 1
  alignment timestamps that drove slicing, not from any generated sound.
- **Never hardcode ComfyUI node IDs.** Every node the orchestrator touches is
  located by `class_type` (with `_meta.title` or wiring as a disambiguator
  where needed) via graph introspection in `workflow_graph.py` — a user
  editing the workflow on the ComfyUI canvas renumbers node IDs freely, and
  the orchestrator must survive that.
- **No sleep-polling.** Execution tracking is entirely event-driven over the
  WebSocket connection; there is no polling loop anywhere in the render path.
- **One chunk failing must not kill the run.** `resilience.py`'s state
  machine classifies failures, retries with backoff, dead-letters a chunk
  after exhausting its attempts, and persists resumable run state after every
  chunk — a run with some dead-lettered chunks still finishes (see
  [Exit codes](#running-a-render) below) rather than aborting.

## Install

Requires **Python 3.10+**.

```bash
pip install -e .
```

The core install is deliberately light — it excludes the heavy ML stack.
Optional extras:

```bash
# Forced alignment (Stage 1) -- pulls stable-ts + torch (~2 GB). Install this
# only on the machine that actually runs alignment; Stage 1's unit tests mock
# the model entirely, so CI and every other stage stay light without it.
pip install -e ".[align]"

# Seed-frame face gate (issue #47) -- pulls opencv-python-headless. Needed only
# on a machine that renders with i2v_continuity on: the chained path has no
# reference-image input, so a seed frame with no face in it carries no identity
# and must not be chained from. The whole test suite exercises the decision
# through an injected predicate and never needs OpenCV.
pip install -e ".[faces]"

# Dev tooling: pytest, pytest-cov, ruff.
pip install -e ".[dev]"
```

**FFmpeg must be installed at the OS level** — it is not a pip dependency.
`pydub` uses it for any non-`.wav` audio I/O, and Stage 5 assembly shells out
to the `ffmpeg` binary directly (e.g. `brew install ffmpeg` on macOS,
`apt install ffmpeg` on Debian/Ubuntu). The orchestrator never bundles or
downloads it.

**The `mvm-author` console script needs the `claude` CLI on `PATH`** — also
not a pip dependency, and not a pip extra either: phase 1 of the [authoring
layer](#authoring-layer-mvm-author) adds zero new Python packages, only a
new entry point in `[project.scripts]`. `music-video-maker` itself never
needs it — only `mvm-author` shells out to a model.

## Quickstart

[`workflow_api.json`](workflow_api.json) and
[`workflow_i2v_api.json`](workflow_i2v_api.json) are committed (issue #18),
so a clean checkout can run end-to-end against any ComfyUI host with the H3
weights installed. Both were authored from ComfyUI's built-in MiniMax H3
templates — see [Render resolution](#render-resolution) for the one default
you should override before a long run.

### Deployment: co-located by default

`comfyui_url` defaults to `http://127.0.0.1:8188` (issue #50) — the
orchestrator assumes it runs on the same machine as ComfyUI unless told
otherwise. That's the simplest shape for a single GPU box: no network hop, no
host-sleep handling to think about (`prevent_host_sleep()` is a deliberate
no-op off macOS, since a headless Linux ComfyUI host doesn't idle-sleep the
way a laptop does), no separate file share to move assets across.

Pointing `comfyui_url` at a remote host — reached over Tailscale, say, never
a raw LAN IP — is a supported override, not a different code path. That's
this project's own setup: a laptop drives the orchestrator against a
dedicated GPU host, which is why the [GPU custody](#gpu-custody) and
["the machine driving the render must stay awake"](#the-machine-driving-the-render-must-stay-awake)
sections below exist — they're the cost of that specific choice, not
something a co-located run needs to think about.

**If your GPU is exclusively this pipeline's** — nothing else ever renders on
it, so there's no card to hand back — you don't need the pause/resume half of
[GPU custody](#gpu-custody) to think about at all: the run asserts the card
is free, renders, and hands it back with `POST /free`. On a card other things
also want, the stopping and restarting of those other things is a manual step
this pipeline deliberately does not automate — see that section.

What a bigger card does *not* remove is the other half: this pipeline still
needs a ComfyUI instance to itself, because `/interrupt` and `/free` act on
the server rather than on your prompt. See
[GPU custody](#gpu-custody) for why.

### Config

Config is TOML. This is `tests/fixtures/config/valid_template.toml` (a real
fixture in this repo), parameterized with the "Lucky Ones" example this
project was designed against — a progressive-rock video whose narrative
concept has an oblivious lead vocalist wandering through a surgery, kicking a
life support plug out while the surgeons scramble:

```toml
master_audio = "audio/master.wav"
lyrics_file = "lyrics.txt"
global_style = "Refestramus progressive rock music video, 35mm film"
narrative_concept = "Wandering through a surgery, kicking a life support plug out, surgeons scrambling"
setting = "London, UK -- contemporary, overcast winter"    # where/when the WHOLE video happens
global_appearance = "everyone slim, trim and healthy looking"   # optional; whole-cast presentation
default_lead_vocalist = "Dianne"
comfyui_url = "http://127.0.0.1:8188"      # optional -- this is the default
workflow_template = "workflow_api.json"
chunks_dir = "output/chunks"
final_video_dir = "output/final"

[cast.Dianne]
role = "Lead Vocalist, smiling constantly, oblivious"
image = "cast/dianne_ref_01.jpg"
appearance = "in her late forties, softly lit"   # optional; this member only

[cast.Rex]
role = "Drummer, background, never sings"
image = "cast/rex_ref.jpg"

[hardware]
name = "RTX 4090"
vram_gb = 24.0
min_chunk_seconds = 5.167    # optional; clamped into H3's trained range
max_chunk_seconds = 15.083   # optional; see "Chunk duration window" below
recommended_nodes = ["SageAttention"]   # optional
```

All paths may be absolute or relative; relative paths resolve against the
*config file's* directory, not the process's working directory. The full,
annotated schema — including the Wave 3 resilience/I2V knobs
(`watchdog_timeout_seconds`, `max_render_attempts`, `retry_backoff_seconds`,
`min_free_disk_gb`, `run_state_file`, `i2v_continuity`,
`i2v_workflow_template`, `resume_ignore_prompt_changes`), all optional with
defaults — is the module
docstring at the top of
[`music_video_maker/config.py`](music_video_maker/config.py); treat that as
the source of truth if this README ever drifts from it.

#### Properties that must hold across the whole video

`setting`, `global_appearance` and a cast member's `appearance` exist because
a property stated in forty separate places drifts. The first full render walked
out of a British terraced street, wheelie bins and sash windows included, into
a snowy park reading as Central Park with US emergency vehicles — every shot
individually plausible, the sequence incoherent, because nothing named a place
and H3 chose per shot from whatever each description implied. `setting` is
composed into *every* prompt, so it cannot drift; the loader warns when it is
missing, and a shot plan naming a landmark from another city warns at load
rather than after the GPU time is spent.

The appearance fields exist for the same structural reason `role` must not say
what a character is doing vocally: `role` is injected into every chunk, so a
second kind of statement smuggled into it applies everywhere too, whether or
not that makes sense. Presentation direction — lighting, styling, apparent age
— is a real and normal piece of music-video craft, so it gets its own field
instead of more free text in `role`. Note the weaker lever, though: H3 follows
the reference photo far more closely than any adjective, so a lightly
retouched `image` will move the likeness more than `appearance` will.

**State an endpoint, never a displacement.** `appearance` reads as `"in her
late forties"`, not `"looking a few years younger"`. A displacement names no
stopping point, so it can always go further — and on the chained render path,
where a shot is seeded from the previous shot's own final frame, it is
re-applied to its own output once per shot. On a real render that turned the
lead into a visibly different, younger woman over each run of chained shots.
The code no longer restates appearance on that path (issue #46), but a
displacement is still the wrong shape of instruction: the reference path
re-reads it against the photo every single chunk.

A cast member never has to be the active vocalist — `Rex` above is a
background drummer who's never `default_lead_vocalist` and never a chunk's
active character; he's just an entry the pipeline can inject into a shot's
prompt if a lyric line ever tags him.

#### Chunk duration window

MiniMax H3's `length` input is a **frame count**, not a duration, quantized
on a fixed grid (24 fps, `5 + 17k` frames — see
[`docs/h3-node-schema.md`](docs/h3-node-schema.md) and
`music_video_maker/contracts.py`'s `FrameGrid`). The model's trained range is
124–362 frames, i.e. **~5.167 s – ~15.083 s**, and that is the window the
pipeline enforces — it replaces an earlier 4–15 s window from before the
frame-grid schema existed.

Stage 2a snaps every chunk's duration onto that grid and slices the audio to
*exactly* that duration, so a chunk's audio-stem duration equals its rendered
video duration by construction. The rounding remainder is absorbed into
adjacent instrumental padding — never by cutting or stretching vocal content.
The chosen frame count rides forward on `AudioChunk.frame_count` and is
injected as the H3 node's `length` at render time.

This matters because an unquantized duration would be rounded by ComfyUI
itself at execution time (up to ±0.354 s per chunk; 17 frames = 0.7083 s)
while the audio stem kept its original duration. Stage 5 muxes the pristine
master over the concatenated result and never re-corrects a per-chunk
mismatch, so that error would accumulate into progressive lip-sync drift
across the song. Both `HardwareProfile`s now derive their window from the
grid; note that a larger card does **not** get a longer maximum, because more
VRAM does not extend what the model was trained on.

#### Render resolution

`render_width` / `render_height` override the H3 node's dimensions for the
run. **This is the single biggest lever on how long a render takes** — cost
scales with the latent volume, so it dominates frame count. Measured on
doris's 4090 at 141 frames:

| Resolution | Per chunk | 4:22 song (~40 chunks) |
|---|---|---|
| 864×480 | ~3.7 min | ~2.7 h |
| 1152×640 | ~6.6 min | ~4.8 h |
| 1344×768 | 9 m 15 s *(measured)* | ~6.8 h |

1344×768 is what ComfyUI's built-in `video_minimax_h3_r2v` template ships
with, and therefore what [`workflow_api.json`](workflow_api.json) was
authored from. Omit both fields to keep whatever the template carries.

Both must be set together — a width alone silently changes the aspect ratio
of every shot against the template's height — and both must be multiples of
32 (H3's declared dimension grid; see
[`docs/h3-node-schema.md`](docs/h3-node-schema.md)). Off-grid values are
rejected at config load rather than silently rounded by ComfyUI at execution.

Render small while iterating on a shot plan; raise it once the video is right.

**VRAM headroom is not a speed lever.** Freeing 5.4 GB by stopping doris's
other GPU tenants changed the same chunk from 554.72 s to 554.78 s. Do not
chase memory looking for speed — change resolution.

#### Text encoder

`text_encoder` pins which text encoder ComfyUI loads for the run — a file name
inside doris's own `models/text_encoders/`, **not** a path on the machine
running the pipeline:

```toml
text_encoder = "qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_int8_convrot.safetensors"
```

Omit it to use whatever the workflow templates name. Either way the resolved
encoder is recorded in every chunk's fingerprint, so a template edited on the
canvas can no longer swap the encoder without `--resume` noticing.

The encoder is a truncated Qwen3-VL-32B (50 layers, no `lm_head`) and it is
the component asked to *understand* the prompt — long compositional sentences
about cause and effect. It is also the component quantised hardest in the
build that fits a 24 GB card. Comfy-Org publishes three precisions of the same
encoder, all loadable by the same core `CLIPLoader` (`type="minimax"`):

| File | Size | Bits |
|---|---|---|
| `qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_nvfp4_awq.safetensors` | 15.7 GB | ~4 |
| `qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_int8_convrot.safetensors` | 27.1 GB | 8 |
| `qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_bf16.safetensors` | 51.5 GB | 16 |

It is not a text-only encoder: the same file carries the Qwen3-VL vision tower
(351 of its 2054 tensors), and `MiniMaxH3ReferenceToVideo` hands the cast
photos to `clip.tokenize()` — so encoder precision touches *identity*, not
only prose comprehension. See issue #39 for the A/B this knob exists to make
possible: hold span, prompt, seed and conditioning stem fixed, change only
this.

Pinning applies to both authored templates (each carries its own
`CLIPLoader`), because a run that swapped the encoder on the base path only
would produce a video whose chained shots were encoded at a different
precision from its sung ones.

#### Chaining shots together

`i2v_continuity = true` lets a chunk render from the previous chunk's final
frame instead of from the cast photo, so consecutive shots don't re-invent the
room. Four knobs govern it, and each exists because of a specific failure:

```toml
i2v_continuity        = true
i2v_workflow_template = "workflow_i2v_api.json"
i2v_chain_scope       = "instrumental"   # "instrumental" | "all" | "none"
i2v_reanchor_interval = 4                # break the chain every N chunks
i2v_require_seed_face = true             # refuse to chain from a faceless frame
```

- **`i2v_chain_scope`** — the chained node (`MiniMaxH3ImageToVideo`) has **no
  audio-conditioning input at all**: no `audio_vae`, no `ref_audios`. A chained
  chunk therefore *cannot lip-sync*. `"instrumental"` (the default) chains
  exactly where that trade is free and keeps every sung line on the
  audio-conditioned base path. `"all"` chains everything and warns loudly about
  what it costs (issue #28).
- **`i2v_reanchor_interval`** — every chained chunk conditions on the previous
  one's output, so drift feeds back and compounds. This breaks the chain on a
  fixed `chunk_id % N` grid, sending that chunk back through the reference path
  where the cast photo and vocal stem re-assert themselves. It is modulo rather
  than a running counter deliberately, so a resumed run produces the same chain
  as the original.
- **`i2v_require_seed_face`** — the chained path has no reference-image input,
  so the seed frame is the *entire* identity conditioning. If the previous shot
  ends on the back of the performer's head, the next shot has nothing to build
  a likeness from and invents one. On a real render that produced a visibly
  different woman when she turned back to camera. The gate detects a face in
  the candidate seed and, if there isn't one big and confident enough, renders
  through the base path instead — which restores the cast photo *and* audio
  conditioning, so the fallback is strictly better conditioned than what it
  replaces. Needs the `faces` extra (issue #47).
- **`i2v_min_seed_face_fraction`** — the area floor, default 0.45% of the
  frame. Calibrated against real seed frames rather than chosen; see
  `music_video_maker/faces.py` for the measurements and for the one thing this
  gate cannot do, which is tell *whose* face it found.

Leave `i2v_continuity = false` for a first run — it needs a second authored
template and is much easier to debug once the base path is known good.

#### Conditioning on an isolated vocal stem

`vocal_stem` points at a vocals-only track and uses it as H3's *conditioning*
audio instead of the full mix. Stage 5 still muxes the pristine master, and the
stem is cut at exactly the spans Stage 2a computed from the master, so nothing
about the timeline changes. On a real render this is what fixed chorus
lip-sync, where dense instrumentation was drowning the vocal the model was
being asked to follow. The file is produced off the render path (e.g.
`python -m demucs --two-stems=vocals`) and listened to before use — never an
inference call inside the pipeline. See
[`docs/vocal-stem-workflow.md`](docs/vocal-stem-workflow.md) (issue #25).

#### Correcting the alignment by hand

When no alignment model gets a passage right, `[[alignment_override]]` tables
pin a segment's span as authored data:

```toml
[[alignment_override]]
segment_index = 6
start = 71.41
end = 76.55
reason = "base piles this phrase at 62-65.4; stem energy, two other models and ears all place it here"
```

`reason` is required — an override is a claim that the machine is wrong, and
the next person to read the file deserves to know why. Overrides are applied
before quality evaluation, so the summary reflects the timeline that will
actually be rendered, and the result is validated as a whole sequence rather
than per edit (issue #42).

#### Shot plan

Without one, every chunk in a run is prompted with the same
`narrative_concept`, which gives a video with no progression — and if that
string names several settings, H3 marches through all of them inside every
chunk. On real footage that also cost most of the lip-sync: exactly one of
the four shots per chunk actually sang, because H3 elects a single shot to be
the performance and lets the others drift off the audio conditioning.

A shot plan is an optional TOML file giving one authored line per chunk:

```toml
[[shot]]
chunk_id = 4
start = 26.333333333333336
shot = "She rounds the end of the office aisle still singing to camera as, behind her, the printer erupts in a fireball"
```

Point `shot_plan = "shot_plan.toml"` at it. Each entry replaces
`narrative_concept` for that chunk only — global style, active cast member
and lyric line are still composed around it — and any chunk without an entry
falls back to the global concept with a warning.

**Planning is an authoring step, not a runtime one.** The plan is written
once and committed as data; nothing in the render path calls an LLM or any
external service, so [`prompting.py`](music_video_maker/prompting.py) stays
pure deterministic string composition and `--resume` re-renders a chunk with
byte-identical text.

`start` is a drift anchor. Re-running alignment against edited lyrics or a
different model reshapes segments and renumbers chunks, at which point shot
12's direction would land on another chunk's audio — and the result still
looks deliberate, which is what makes it dangerous. A mismatch beyond 0.25 s
raises `ShotPlanDriftError` and stops the run.

##### Generating a skeleton with `--prepare`

Writing a plan by hand means reading `chunk_id`/`start`/the lyric back out of
a render log and retyping them, once per chunk. `--prepare` runs Stages 1-2
only (~6 s, no GPU, no ComfyUI, no custody handoff) and writes a
`shot_plan.toml` skeleton with every field filled in except `shot`:

```bash
music-video-maker --config run.toml --prepare
```

```toml
[[shot]]
chunk_id = 4
start = 26.333333333333336   # 26.333 - 32.208  (5.875s, 141 frames)
# lyric: "and the lights flicker but i do not mind"
shot = ""

[[shot]]
chunk_id = 5
start = 32.208333333333336   # 32.208 - 42.333  (10.125s, 243 frames)
# INSTRUMENTAL -- no lyric to sing
shot = ""
```

`chunk_id` and `start` come straight from the alignment that produced them,
so a skeleton loaded back against the same run can never drift — write the
`shot` lines, nothing else. A blank `shot = ""` is not a malformed entry: it
loads exactly like a missing one and falls back to `narrative_concept`, so a
half-finished plan is still a runnable plan. Defaults to writing
`shot_plan.toml` next to `--config`; override with `--shot-plan-out`.
`--prepare` refuses to overwrite an existing file — an authored plan is real
work — pass `--force` to overwrite deliberately.

**If your plan sets any `length_seconds`, re-prepare it with `--from-plan`.**
An editorial shot length re-tiles the timeline: the chunk at that anchor
swallows the ones it now covers, and every chunk after it gets a new id and a
new start. A skeleton generated *before* those lengths therefore describes
chunks no render will ever produce, and the first affected entry raises
`ShotPlanDriftError`.

```bash
music-video-maker --config run.toml --prepare --from-plan shot_plan.toml \
                  --shot-plan-out shot_plan_v2.toml
```

That reads the existing plan **for its `length_seconds` only** — no shot text,
no camera direction — re-slices the way a render with those lengths will, and
emits the anchors that render actually produces. Copy your `shot` lines onto
it and the plan is self-consistent: the same lengths at those same anchors
re-cut to exactly that timeline. A plan it cannot read fails the whole
prepare rather than quietly falling back to a length-free skeleton, which
would look like success and drift hours later on the GPU.

#### Cinematography and camera direction

Film-direction language — stock, lens, depth of field, lighting, camera
movement — used to have nowhere to live except `global_style` (the whole
film's look) and free text inside `shot` (this scene's action), which made
`global_style` a junk drawer for genre, tone, film stock, lighting and
editing continuity all in one string, and left camera movement's sentence
position up to whoever wrote the shot line.

```toml
cinematography = "35mm film, shallow depth of field, warm natural light"
```

```toml
[[shot]]
chunk_id = 4
start = 26.333333333333336
shot   = "She rounds the end of the office aisle as the printer erupts in a fireball"
camera = "tracking backwards ahead of her"
```

`cinematography` (config-level, optional) is the whole-video look, composed
right after `global_style` on every chunk. `camera` (per-shot, in the shot
plan beside `shot`) is this shot's own framing and movement — write it the
way a hand-written trailing camera phrase already reads ("tracking backwards
ahead of her"), assuming "camera" as the implied subject.

The reason this is a field rather than more free text in `shot` is
[the guide's grammatical-subject rule](docs/shot-writing-guide.md#make-the-effect-the-grammatical-subject):
H3 renders the sentence's grammatical subject and largely drops what sits in
a subordinate clause. `camera` is always composed as a **trailing clause** on
the shot/concept sentence — `"<shot>, camera <direction>"` — never as its own
sentence, so it can never take the subject slot away from whatever the shot
is actually about. Both fields are optional and independent of each other and
of `shot`; a config written before they existed keeps rendering unchanged.

#### Your first real run

Copy [`examples/first-run.toml`](examples/first-run.toml) rather than the
snippet above — it is the same config with one deliberate change:

```toml
[hardware]
max_chunk_seconds = 8.0
```

H3's trained ceiling is 15.083 s (362 frames), and that is what the profiles
default to. **Cap your first run well under it.** What is actually proven on
doris is ~124–141-frame (5–6 s) clips at good quality; nothing longer has been
rendered there at any resolution. Temporal VAE decode memory scales
*non-linearly* with frame count, so 362 frames is roughly 3× the frame count
anyone has tested and is the likeliest thing to fail on a first attempt.

On **timing**, see [Render resolution](#render-resolution) below rather than
frame count: a chunk's cost is set far more by how many pixels it renders than
by how many frames. 141 frames measured 9 m 15 s at 1344×768 and roughly 3 min
at 864×480.

8.0 s is not a round number chosen for tidiness — it is exactly **192 frames
(5 + 17×11)**, landing precisely on the `length` grid. The window it opens is
124 / 141 / 158 / 175 / 192 frames: five valid chunk lengths, none far outside
proven territory.

If chunks dead-letter with CUDA OOM, *lower* this before reaching for anything
else — fewer frames per chunk costs nothing and needs no code change. Raising
it should follow a measurement, not a hope.

Since this was written, 226-frame chunks (9.4 s) have rendered successfully on
doris at 864×480, so the proven ceiling has moved. It is still well short of
H3's 362, and the reason to be careful is specific: when H3 stages onto a
contended card it does not reliably raise CUDA OOM — it can go silent mid-load
and wedge the host
([#24](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/24)).

Also leave `i2v_continuity = false` for a first run. It needs a second
authored template and is easier to debug once the base path is known good —
one unknown at a time.

### Lyrics

`tests/fixtures/lyrics/tagged.txt` is a real fixture, but the words in it are
invented for the test — they are *not* the lyrics of "The Lucky Ones" or of any
other real song. It exists to show the tag syntax:

```
[Dianne: Lead]
The lucky ones don't ever have to try
[Marcus: Backup]
We're watching from the wings tonight
[Dianne: Lead]
But I'm the one who taught you how to fly
[Marcus: Backup]
And I'm the one who watched you go
[Dianne: Lead]
So catch me if the lights go down below
```

Note this fixture tags a `Marcus`, who isn't in the `valid_template.toml`
cast shown above (that fixture only defines `Dianne` and `Rex` — it's used by
a different test). To run this exact lyric file against that exact config,
either add a `[cast.Marcus]` entry or swap `Marcus` for `Rex`/`Dianne` in the
lyrics. See [`docs/lyrics-format.md`](docs/lyrics-format.md) for the full tag
syntax and the stripping rule that keeps tags out of forced alignment.

#### An entirely instrumental video

Leave `lyrics_file` empty (or point it at a file with no non-blank lines) and
the whole track renders as instrumental: `align()` returns zero segments, and
`instrumental_coverage` (on by default) tiles the *entire* track as filler
chunks rather than producing nothing. Every chunk's prompt gets the
"performs silently, no lyric to sing" clause — never "actively singing" —
because that clause is driven purely by whether a chunk carries lyric text,
not by whether the audio has vocals in it.

This is also how to render a video where the master audio *does* have real
vocals but nobody is ever shown singing: point `lyrics_file` at an empty
file anyway. Stage 5 still muxes the pristine `master_audio` — real vocals
included — over the final video; an empty `lyrics_file` only changes what's
*shown*, never what's heard. `default_lead_vocalist` still has to name a
real cast member either way: `MiniMaxH3ReferenceToVideo` always needs a
reference photo to condition every shot on, so the field is really "whose
face anchors any chunk nobody tagged" rather than "who sings" — the two only
look identical when every song has a visible singer.

### Running a render

```bash
music-video-maker --config path/to/run.toml
```

Flags (see `music_video_maker/cli.py`'s `build_parser()`):

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--config PATH` | Required. Path to the run config TOML file. |
| `--resume` | Resume a partial run using the persisted `RunState` at `run_state_file`. Chunks previously `RENDERED`/`CACHED` are reused as `CACHED` *only if* their video file still exists on disk **and** the fingerprint they were rendered for still matches this run's (see "Resume proves identity" below); chunks previously `DEAD_LETTERED` are always retried. |
| `--ignore-prompt-changes` | With `--resume`, reuse chunks whose span is unchanged but whose *prompt* was edited — a shot-plan tweak, a reworded cast role. Chunks whose span, frame count or render resolution moved are re-rendered regardless. One-way; the config equivalent is `resume_ignore_prompt_changes`. |
| `--strict-alignment` | Refuse to render when forced alignment produces implausible timings — zero-length segments, a lyric placed where nothing is sung, a line split across a huge gap (see below). Report-only without it. One-way; the config equivalent is `strict_alignment`. |
| `--only-chunks IDS` | Render only these chunk ids (`32,33,34,35`) and skip Stage 5 assembly. Stages 1–2 still run over the *whole* track, so each chunk gets exactly the span, prompt and frame count a full run would give it — re-slicing around a selection would validate a chunk the real run never produces. This is the validation slice: prove a prompt-shape or gate change on a few chunks before committing hours of exclusive GPU custody. A slice loads prior run state and augments it, and always re-renders its own chunks even when their fingerprints still match. |
| `--log-level LEVEL` | Root log level, default `INFO`. All diagnostic output goes to stderr (`logging_setup.py`) — there is no separate progress bar; the WebSocket `progress` events logged at `INFO` *are* the progress display. |

Exit codes (`music_video_maker/cli.py`):

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `0` | Success — every chunk rendered or was reused from cache, and the final video was assembled. |
| `1` | Error — config failed to load/validate, or the pipeline run raised an unhandled exception. |
| `2` | Partial failure — the run finished, but one or more chunks were dead-lettered after exhausting `max_render_attempts`. Stage 5 assembly is skipped entirely in this case (a video assembled with a chunk missing would silently desync against the master track), so the run's chunk videos are on disk but no final video exists yet. Fix the dead-lettered chunk(s) (bad reference image, template issue, etc.) and `--resume`. |

#### Alignment quality is reported on every run

Forced alignment can fail badly while raising nothing. On a real track it
produced a zero-length segment, five words inside 70 ms, one lyric line split
across a 38-second gap, and a lyric placed at 3:55 in a passage whose last
vocal is at 3:16 — and the run log recorded none of it. stable-ts's own
`1/27 segments failed to align` warning was discarded too, because the
`warnings` module was never routed into logging.

Both are fixed: stable-ts's warnings now reach the log, and every run logs an
alignment-quality summary at INFO covering zero-length and near-zero segments,
implausible words-per-second in both directions, out-of-order and overlapping
segments, large gaps inside one lyric line, segments isolated from all vocal
activity around them, and stable-ts's own per-word confidence where it exposes
it. `--strict-alignment` turns a critical finding into a refusal.

It is report-only by default on purpose — a real song always has some odd
segments, so refusing by default would be wrong. But alignment takes about six
seconds and a render takes hours, so for an unattended run `--strict-alignment`
is cheap insurance against spending the card on a timeline already known to be
broken. Note also what a bad timeline *looks* like downstream: a hallucinated
lyric in an instrumental passage is classified as voiced, gets a lyric, and is
prompted to sing — a third, independent cause of "the performer sings during
the instrumental break".

#### Resume proves identity, it doesn't assume it

`--resume` used to reuse a cached chunk on the strength of its `chunk_id` and
the mp4 still being on disk. That says the chunk *exists*; it does not say it
is the *right* chunk. Anything that moves the chunk timeline — editing the
lyrics file, changing the alignment `model_size` or the chunk duration bounds,
toggling `instrumental_coverage`, re-trimming the master audio — leaves every
mp4 and the whole `run_state.json` superficially valid while the spans they
were rendered for no longer exist. Stage 5 would then concat clips that each
look fine into a video that is silently desynced against the master track,
with no error anywhere.

So every result in `run_state.json` now records a **fingerprint** of what it
was rendered for, and a resumed run compares it (issue #34):

- **Span, frame count, render resolution** — a mismatch means the clip belongs
  somewhere else in the song (or, for resolution, that Stage 5's `-c:v copy`
  concat cannot join it at all). Always re-rendered. No flag overrides this.
- **Prompt, active character, reference photo, noise seed, chain source** — the
  clip is in the right place but no longer reflects the config. Re-rendered by
  default; set `resume_ignore_prompt_changes = true` (or pass
  `--ignore-prompt-changes`) to reuse it as-is, which is what you want when a
  shot-plan typo fix would otherwise cost a full re-render. The noise seed
  (issue #38) is here because a re-seeded chunk is a *different take* of the
  right moment, not a misplaced one.
- **Conditioning audio, text encoder, and the graph itself** — mix vs isolated
  stem (issue #25), which encoder read the prompt (issue #39), and a hash of
  the workflow template the chunk actually rendered through (issue #45). Its
  own tier: the clip is in the right place, but each of these is the
  *experiment variable* of an A/B this project actually runs, so reusing a
  chunk across the swap would score the control against itself. A template edit
  is not a prompt typo either. Always re-rendered;
  `resume_ignore_prompt_changes` does not reach it.

Two asymmetries worth knowing. A run holds **two** templates and a chunk
renders through exactly one, so the recorded hash is of the graph *that chunk*
used. And a chunk that was *planned* to chain but degraded to the base path —
because its predecessor dead-lettered, its frame would not extract, or the face
gate refused the seed — records no chain and the base graph. That is a
degradation, not a config change, and is reused rather than re-rendered on
every resume forever.

A mismatch is logged with the field that changed and both values. When *most*
chunks moved — the shape of a lyrics edit, which shifts everything after it —
one summary line is logged instead of one warning per chunk, and the per-chunk
detail drops to `DEBUG`.

`run_state.json` carries a `schema_version` for this reason. A state file
written before fingerprints existed records nothing that can be proven, so it
is rejected outright and the run starts fresh rather than being misread as
"everything matches".

### GPU custody

There are two different exclusivity requirements here, and only one of them
is about how big your card is.

**Exclusive custody of the ComfyUI instance is unconditional.** No amount of
VRAM removes it, because the endpoints this pipeline relies on are
server-global rather than per-prompt:

- `POST /interrupt` takes no `prompt_id` — it interrupts whatever that server
  is currently executing. The resilience recovery sequence fires it on any
  transient failure, so your retry kills a co-tenant's job.
- `POST /free` with `unload_models` unloads *everyone's* models. It runs on
  every recovery and once more at the end of the run.
- The queue is serial and the watchdog is wall-clock. Another client's prompt
  queued ahead of yours can time out your chunk, which then interrupts their
  job — compounding the first two.

So run this pipeline against a ComfyUI process it owns for the duration.
Two renders sharing one ComfyUI is a correctness problem, not a performance
one.

**Exclusive custody of the card is a consequence of the card being small**,
not a property of rendering. A 24 GB 4090 holding H3's ~20 GB has no room for
a co-tenant, and the failure mode is expensive: when H3 stages onto a
contended card it does not reliably raise CUDA OOM — it can go silent mid-load
and wedge the host
([#24](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/24)). Everything
below exists to keep a run from ever reaching that edge. On a card with
genuine headroom for H3 *plus* whatever else lives there, none of it is
needed: the pre-flight and the `POST /free` afterwards cost nothing and are
the parts that generalise.

#### Stopping the other tenants is yours, not the pipeline's

The pipeline does not stop or start anything else that uses your GPU. That is
deliberate. Which processes hold the card is specific to a machine and changes
over time, and an automated "pause the other thing" step is somebody's
personal infrastructure shipped as everyone's default — this repo carried one
of those for months, wired in and enabled by default, and it never ran once
outside its own tests.

What the pipeline does instead is refuse to start when the card is not
actually free, and re-check between chunks. So the protocol is:

1. **Before the run:** stop whatever else uses the GPU. Enumerate it rather
   than trusting a list — `nvidia-smi
   --query-compute-apps=pid,process_name,used_memory --format=csv`, and
   `systemctl list-timers --all` for anything scheduled that could arrive
   mid-render.
2. **Start the run.** The pre-flight refuses it outright if the card is still
   held (`min_free_vram_gb`, default 16 GB).
3. **After the run:** the pipeline `POST /free`s ComfyUI's models. Restart
   whatever you stopped.

The part worth internalising: **stopping the one tenant you know about is not
the same as freeing the card.** See the known gap
[below](#known-gaps-please-read-before-filing-a-this-doesnt-work-issue).

#### The machine driving the render must stay awake

A render is hours of work this process only *supervises* — nearly all of its
life is spent blocked on a WebSocket, which reads to macOS as an idle machine.
On 2026-08-11 the host slept mid-chunk and froze the orchestrator for three and
a half hours at a time while doris carried on finishing prompts nobody was
listening for; nothing failed, the run just stopped advancing.

Two things now prevent that costing anything:

- **The run holds a sleep assertion for its duration** (`caffeinate -dims -w
  <pid>`, macOS only), taken and released like the custody handoff beside it.
  It is
  tied to this process id, so it dies with the run even on `SIGKILL` and can
  never leave a machine pinned awake. A host without `caffeinate` is warned
  about, not refused.
- **A lost socket is no longer read as a lost render.** When the connection
  drops, the client asks `/history/{prompt_id}` before writing the attempt off,
  and harvests a video ComfyUI already finished instead of re-rendering it.
- **The watchdog is bounded at the socket level.** A sleeping process gets no
  CPU, so `recv()` never times out on our schedule, and macOS's monotonic clock
  does not advance across sleep either — what actually ended the 3.5-hour wait
  was the OS giving up on the TCP connection at its own default. TCP keepalive
  is now configured from `watchdog_timeout_seconds`, putting worst-case dead-peer
  detection at about 1.33× the configured window instead of roughly 14×.

None of it helps if the lid closes — clamshell sleep overrides every assertion,
and keepalive probes come from a network stack that is itself suspended, so
detection fires on *wake* rather than during the outage. For a long
unattended run, the durable fix is [#50](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/50):
run the orchestrator co-located with ComfyUI instead of on a laptop that can
sleep. All of this section's mitigations exist specifically for the case
where that isn't done — a shared, remote GPU host reached over Tailscale, the
way this project's own runs work.

## Authoring layer (`mvm-author`)

*All four phases of [#54](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/54)
— concept, beats, photography and prose, ending in a `shot_plan.toml` a human
reviews and renders. Validated by re-authoring "The Lucky Ones" from scratch
against the real CLI; see
[Known gaps](#known-gaps-please-read-before-filing-a-this-doesnt-work-issue)
for what that showed it still cannot do.*

`music-video-maker` never calls a model — that is the entire point of
[pure string composition](#non-negotiable-invariants). `mvm-author` is a
**separate binary** that does, on purpose: it reads a song's real structure
(the same Stage 1-2 alignment/slicing `--prepare` runs) and proposes a
narrative concept, then a beat sheet, then the shot lines themselves —
through the Claude CLI, never a metered API. Its job ends at a
`shot_plan.toml` on disk that a human reads, edits and commits; nothing it
produces is ever fed back into a render automatically.

```bash
mvm-author --config run.toml concept --dry-run   # print the exact prompt, call nothing
mvm-author --config run.toml concept             # call the model, write .authoring/concept.json
mvm-author --config run.toml concept --notes "too bleak, and no rain"   # targeted re-roll
mvm-author --config run.toml beats               # what happens in each chunk -> .authoring/beats.json
mvm-author --config run.toml beats --notes "the printer gag needs more room"
mvm-author --config run.toml photography --candidates 3   # three looks, side by side
mvm-author --config run.toml photography --pick 2         # freeze one, no model call
mvm-author --config run.toml prose               # the shot lines -> .authoring/prose.json
mvm-author --config run.toml prose --groups 3-5 --notes "stop putting her in doorways"
mvm-author --config run.toml write               # compose, lint, revise -> shot_plan.toml
mvm-author --config run.toml all                 # every stage, stopping for approval
mvm-author --config run.toml status              # what's been generated, and whether it's stale
```

Session state — `.authoring/session.json`, `concept.json`, `beats.json`,
`photography.json`, `prose.json`, the raw model replies — lives beside the run config in
`~/mvm-runs/<song>/`, exactly like `output/` and `shot_plan.toml`, never in
the repo.

#### What the beats stage is for

It does not write prose. It says *what happens* in each chunk and tags it:

```json
{"chunk_id": 12, "beat": "the printer erupts", "beat_role": "consequence",
 "beat_group": 3, "focus": "action", "length_seconds": 9.0}
```

`beat_role` (`plant` / `contact` / `consequence` / `transition` /
`instrumental`) and `beat_group` are what earn this stage its own model call:
they make three of
[`docs/shot-writing-guide.md`](docs/shot-writing-guide.md)'s checklist items
machine-checkable **before a word of prose exists** — every consequence needs
a plant and a contact earlier in its own group, every consequence carries
`focus = "action"`, and a lone consequence is a cause and its effect
compressed into one shot. A sheet that breaks any of them goes straight back
to the model with every problem listed at once.

Anchors are never taken from the model: a `chunk_id` that is not in the
skeleton is dropped, and `start`/`end` are re-emitted from the skeleton every
time — which is what makes `ShotPlanDriftError` structurally unreachable for
a generated plan rather than merely unlikely.

If the sheet asks for any `length_seconds`, the timeline is re-cut with those
requests and every beat is **re-anchored by song time** onto the result — the
authoring-side twin of `--prepare --from-plan` above. A long take that
swallows several beats merges them in chunk order, keeping `focus = "action"`
if any of them was a consequence. A chunk that ends up with no beat is
reported and sent back to the model as work to redo, never left blank: blank
falls through to the global `narrative_concept`, which renders as something
deliberate-looking that nobody authored.

#### What the prose stage is for, and what `write` checks

Prose turns each beat into the `shot` line, **windowed on beat groups** —
never a fixed chunk count, because a window boundary falling between a
contact and its consequence produces exactly the defect the three-beat rule
exists to prevent. Each window is written with the two chunks either side as
read-only context, so a payoff is always written by a call that has seen its
plant. Groups are also the unit a human objects in, which is why `--groups`
takes them: everything outside the ones you name keeps the prose you already
approved.

`write` then composes the TOML and puts it through **the real loaders** —
`load_shot_plan`, `shot_length_requests`, `resolve_shot`/`resolve_camera`
over every chunk, and `lint_shots_against_lyrics` — not a reimplementation of
them. Those lints encode measured lessons, and a second copy would drift from
them within a month. Two tiers come out, and they are deliberately not
symmetric:

- **Errors** (a blank line, a malformed entry, drift) get a *targeted*
  revision of just the offending chunks, bounded at two rounds. Then it
  aborts and writes nothing.
- **Warnings** get exactly one round, and whatever survives is written into
  the file as a `# lint:` comment above its entry. They are not ground to
  zero on purpose: every one of these lints is documented as a heuristic
  firing on prose a human wrote deliberately, and a loop that retried until
  they went quiet would happily rewrite a correct shot to please one.

`chunk_id` and `start` are copied from the chunks, never from anything a
model said, so a generated plan loaded back against the run it was generated
for cannot raise `ShotPlanDriftError`. The file also carries a `[provenance]`
block (which model wrote which stage, and **hashes** of what they read —
never the content, since anything committed ships) plus a `content_sha256`
per entry, which is how `status` can tell you afterwards which lines a human
has since edited.

#### `status`

`status` reports each stage as `ok`, `STALE` (an input — the lyrics file, the
chunk skeleton, a doc fed to the model, or an upstream stage's output —
changed since it last ran), or `not started`, and names any generated shot
line that has been edited since. Re-rolling the concept marks `beats` stale
and re-running `beats` marks `prose` stale, because each stage records a hash
of what it descended from; that is the whole cascade mechanism, not a
separate dependency graph. Staleness is only ever *reported*, never
auto-healed: regenerating automatically would throw away a human's approval
of the previous output to satisfy a hash, the same reasoning
`ChunkFingerprint` already uses for render-time `--resume`.

The render/authoring boundary is enforced mechanically, not just by
convention — `tests/test_authoring_boundary.py` parses every module's AST
and fails the suite if anything outside `music_video_maker/authoring/` ever
imports it, or if `authoring/` reaches into the render half through anything
other than a small named allowlist. See
[`music_video_maker/authoring/__init__.py`](music_video_maker/authoring/__init__.py)'s
docstring for the full design.

## Known gaps (please read before filing a "this doesn't work" issue)

- **The 864×480 / 1344×768 gap.** [`workflow_api.json`](workflow_api.json)
  was authored from ComfyUI's built-in template and carries its 1344×768
  default, while hands-on testing on doris was done at 864×480 — a change
  that lived only in a canvas session. That is a 3.1× difference in run time
  (see [Render resolution](#render-resolution)), so set `render_width` /
  `render_height` explicitly rather than inheriting the template's.
- **A quiet card is not proof of a free card.** If anything else can use your
  GPU, pausing the tenant you know about frees only that tenant — desktop
  sessions, other services and anything on a timer hold VRAM independently,
  and the list changes over time. Enumerate it (`nvidia-smi
  --query-compute-apps=pid,process_name,used_memory --format=csv`, `systemctl
  list-timers --all`) rather than trusting a list in any document, including
  this one. The custody pre-flight refuses a run rather than starting one that
  would OOM partway through, and the runner re-reads free VRAM before every
  chunk it submits — on this project's host the largest tenant loads *on
  demand*, so a card that reads empty at the pre-flight is not one that stays
  empty. See
  [`docs/doris-gpu-setup.md`](docs/doris-gpu-setup.md#gpu-custody-issue-19)
  for the worked example.
- **The VRAM guard assumes one GPU.** `custody._extract_free_vram_gb` takes
  the `max()` of free VRAM across ComfyUI's reported `devices[]`, so on a
  multi-GPU host it reads the *freest* card while ComfyUI renders on whichever
  one it was configured for. The pre-flight can therefore pass on headroom
  that the render will never get. Single-GPU hosts are unaffected; that is the
  only shape this has been run on.
- **Detection is not recognition.** The seed-face gate above can tell you a
  face is present; it cannot tell you it is the performer's. In a frame with
  other people in it, somebody else's face passes while the lead has her back
  turned. See
  [#49](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/49).
- **A generated plan is a draft, not an approval, and it has never been
  rendered.** `mvm-author`
  ([#54](https://github.com/JavaDerek/music-video-pipeline/issues/54)) has been
  validated by re-authoring "The Lucky Ones" from scratch and comparing
  against the hand-written 36-entry plan, but no generated plan has been
  through a GPU. Everything below is measured on the *text*, not on frames.
- **The lints cannot catch flat writing.** They catch structural defects.
  The first real run produced prose that passed every check and read as a
  template — "in the foreground" in 24 of 36 shots where the human used it in
  5 — which was fixed in the prompt, not by a lint, because no lint could
  have seen it. Read the shot lines before committing GPU time to them; that
  is what the review loop and `--groups` are for, and the `# lint:` comments
  in the file are advisory, with some false positives by design.
- **`cinematography` is proposed, not applied.** The photography stage
  proposes a look, but it is a `run.toml` value rather than a shot-plan
  field, so `write` prints it for you to copy rather than setting it. Only
  the per-shot `camera` values reach the plan.
